The Dangers of Artificial Intelligence with Alara

AI systems are becoming more capable, more widespread and more embedded in daily life. Alongside real benefits come real risks — some already here, others still on the horizon.

Key risks

01

Misinformation

Convincing fake text, images, audio and video at scale.

Deepfakes and synthetic content can erode trust in what we see and hear, disrupt elections, and make it harder to tell truth from fabrication.

02

Bias & discrimination

Models can absorb and amplify unfair patterns in their data.

When used in hiring, lending, policing or healthcare, biased systems can quietly disadvantage groups of people while appearing objective.
Tap to expand
03

Privacy & surveillance

Powerful tools for tracking, profiling and identifying people.

Facial recognition and large-scale data analysis can enable monitoring of individuals and populations far beyond what was previously possible.
Tap to expand
04

Job disruption

Automation may displace work faster than societies adapt.

Cognitive and creative tasks are increasingly automatable, raising questions about income, retraining and how economic gains are shared.
Tap to expand
05

Security & misuse

AI can lower the barrier for scams, cyberattacks and worse.

Bad actors may use AI to automate phishing, find software vulnerabilities, or seek help with dangerous weapons — making safeguards essential.
Tap to expand
06

Loss of control

Advanced systems might pursue goals we didn't intend.

As AI becomes more autonomous, ensuring it reliably does what humans want — the "alignment problem" — becomes one of the field's central challenges.
Tap to expand
07

Concentration of power

A few actors could gain outsized economic or political influence.

Frontier AI requires enormous resources, which may concentrate capability in a handful of companies or governments with limited accountability.

08

Over-reliance

Trusting AI outputs without checking can cause real harm.

AI can be confidently wrong. Dependence on it may also weaken human skills, judgment and critical thinking over time.

A spectrum of concern

Present harmsBias, scams, deepfakes and privacy violations happening today.
Near-term risksLabour disruption, cyber threats and erosion of shared information.
Long-term risksHighly autonomous systems acting beyond meaningful human oversight.

What can be done

  • Invest in safety and alignment research alongside capability research.
  • Build transparency, testing and independent auditing into AI development.
  • Develop thoughtful regulation and international cooperation.
  • Improve public AI literacy so people can question what they see.
  • Keep humans accountable for high-stakes decisions.
The goal isn't to fear AI, but to shape it — so its benefits are broad and its risks are managed.
